    Questions

    Which end of a resistor do I read from?
    Start from the end where the bands are bunched together. The tolerance band, usually gold or silver on 4-band parts, sits on its own with a bigger gap. If you can't tell, try both ways: the reading that lands on a standard E12 or E24 value is almost always the right one, and Resistor Lab tells you when a value is standard.
    Why does the LED tool round up instead of to the nearest value?
    Rounding the resistor up keeps the current at or below what you asked for, so the LED never runs hotter than planned. The tool shows the actual current you'll get with the standard part, and picks a power rating with at least twice the heat the resistor will make.
    What's the difference between E12, E24 and E96?
    They're the standard value series: 12, 24 or 96 values per decade, spaced so each value's tolerance meets the next. E12 suits 10% parts and is what most kits contain, E24 suits 5% parts, and E96 is for 1% precision resistors.
    Why does my divider read lower once I connect something?
    Whatever you connect sits in parallel with R2, which lowers the bottom resistance and pulls Vout down. Turn on the load option in the divider tool to see by how much. Keeping the load at least ten times R2 holds the error under about 10%.
    What does 472J mean on a capacitor?
    The first two digits are the value, the third is how many zeros to add, in picofarads: 47 and two zeros is 4,700 pF, or 4.7 nF. The letter is the tolerance, and J means ±5%. The capacitor tab decodes these and works the other way too.
